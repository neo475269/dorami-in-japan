35

Kurashiki: Bikan old town

倉敷美観地区

Kurashiki's Bikan quarter feels like stepping into a postcard somebody forgot to update — the canal runs slow and green under the willow trees, and the white-walled storehouses stand shoulder to shoulder along the water like they have been gossiping for three hundred years. I got there just after sunrise, when the light was still soft and the only sound was a boatman's oar dipping into the water.

I leaned on the stone bridge and watched the little wooden boat glide past, its two passengers waving like royalty. The boatman tipped his hat at me, and for a second I felt like a local — until I tried to say good morning in Japanese and accidentally thanked him for the weather instead. He laughed kindly, which is the universal language for “nice try, foreigner.”

By mid-morning the lanes smelled of fresh coffee from a tiny kissaten, and I sat by the window with a cup, watching the willows sway over the canal. There is something about Kurashiki that makes you want to slow down and stay a little longer — so I did, and ordered a second coffee.
