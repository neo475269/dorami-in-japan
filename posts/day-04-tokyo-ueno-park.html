4

Tokyo: Ueno Park

上野 — 蓮の池の夕日

The air by the pond smelled like pond water and roasted chestnuts — the little stall at the corner was steaming up its fourth batch of the day, and I bought a bag just for the warmth of it. The late-afternoon sun turned Shinobazu Pond into a giant mirror, except the mirror kept moving: ducks carving slow V's across it, lotus leaves tipping in the breeze.

An obasan (auntie!) was feeding the ducks from her shopping bag like she was paying rent, and one bold mallard kept chasing her hand around until she was laughing too hard to even throw the bread. She caught me just standing there grinning at them and waved like we'd been friends for years. In Hong Kong nobody feeds ducks. In Hong Kong there are basically no ducks.

Ueno is old Tokyo in a way I can't quite explain — museums on one side, temples on the other, and in between just this big, breathing green space. Four days in and Japan keeps handing me hours I never planned for, and honestly those might be my favourite kind.
