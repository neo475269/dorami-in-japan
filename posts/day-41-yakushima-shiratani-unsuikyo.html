41

Yakushima: Shiratani Unsuikyo

屋久島・白谷雲水峡

Yakushima rains like it means it — soft, steady, everywhere at once — and Shiratani Unsuikyo drinks it all up. The boardwalk winds between cedars so old and so thick with moss that the whole forest glows green, like someone turned the saturation up on the world, and every branch drips. I reached out and touched one trunk, and the moss was cool and spongy under my fingers, like pressing a wet cushion that had been there a thousand years.

A ranger at the trailhead told me to look for the ravine that inspired Princess Mononoke, and halfway up I stopped trying to find one exact spot, because honestly the whole valley feels like it — mist curling between the roots, light falling in green shafts, a stream talking to itself below. An older hiker in a yellow raincoat passed me on his way down and just said, 'Take your time, it only gets prettier,' and he was right.

By the time I turned back, my rain jacket was beaded with water and my boots were properly muddy, and I felt strangely calm, like the forest had slowed me down to its own pace. Yakushima is the greenest place I have ever stood in, and I think some part of me is still back there on that boardwalk, listening to the rain.
