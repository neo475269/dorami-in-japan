7

Tokyo: Mt. Takao

高尾山 — 紅葉登山

The trail smelled like cold cedar and roasted sweet potato drifting up from the teahouse at the base — maple leaves kept landing on the stone steps like someone sprinkling confetti. My legs stopped complaining somewhere around the 800th step, which honestly counts as character growth for a girl raised on escalators.

Halfway up, an ojii-san in his seventies overtook me without breaking a sweat, then turned around, grinned, and mimed pushing me up the last stretch. I laughed so hard I had to stop climbing. He bowed, I bowed, and right then the Yakuoin temple bell rang out through the red maples.

Standing in front of that old red gate with autumn burning all around it, I felt ridiculously lucky. Tokyo can be all neon and noise — but it’s also this: a mountain, a temple, and a stranger cheering you uphill. Seven days down, and I think I’d follow this trail anywhere.
