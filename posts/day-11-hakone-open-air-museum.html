Hakone: Open-Air Museum

箱根彫刻の森 — 紅葉に映える巨大彫刻

I came to the Hakone Open-Air Museum fully intending to behave like a proper art person — quiet footsteps, thoughtful nodding. That lasted about four minutes, because the sculptures here are huge, loud, brightly coloured giants, and the autumn wind was doing cartwheels across the lawn. A maple leaf landed in my hair and I gave up on dignity entirely.

A little boy in a yellow raincoat — it wasn't even raining — tugged my sleeve and asked me to help find his lost football. We found it wedged under a giant blue-and-orange sculpture, and he celebrated like we'd just won the World Cup. Then he bowed so deeply his hood flopped over his eyes, and his mum and I both had to sit down laughing.

Something about this place made everyone act like kids again — grown men posing with silly statues, old couples giggling on the grass. Eleven days in, and Japan keeps reminding me that the best days are never the perfectly planned ones.
