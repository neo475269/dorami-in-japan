29

Miyajima: Itsukushima torii

宮島・厳島神社

I timed the sunset almost badly on purpose — I arrived just as the tide was coming in, and the great torii stood right in the sea, glowing orange against a sky that kept changing its mind between pink and gold. The water smelled of salt and cold air, and every wave lapped a little closer to the stone steps where I was standing.

A kind obaachan selling grilled oysters nearby saw me shivering and pressed a paper cup of hot amazake into my hands, waving away my coins with a laugh. “Cold hands, warm heart,” she said in English, very proudly, and I nearly hugged her on the spot.

Standing there with the warm cup in my hands and the torii turning red in the last light, I felt ridiculously lucky — like Japan keeps handing me these postcard moments and then insisting I stay a little longer to enjoy them.
