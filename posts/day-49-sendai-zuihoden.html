DAY
49

Sendai: Zuihoden

仙台・瑞鳳殿

Zuihoden glows. That is the only word for it — the gold leaf on the black lacquer catches the winter light so hard it looks lit from inside, and the whole mausoleum sits up the hill among the cedars like a jewel somebody left in the forest. I climbed the stone steps slowly, partly out of respect and partly because they were dusted with snow and I did not trust my boots.

Halfway up I stopped to warm my hands around a can of coffee from the station vending machine, and an old man sweeping snow off the steps nodded at me like we were colleagues. “Date Masamune,” he said, pointing up at the gold doors with his broom, and then went back to sweeping as if that explained everything. It kind of did — a warlord who wanted his resting place to shine.

Standing there under the cedars, with the smell of cold pine and incense in the air, I felt properly small in the good way. Sendai does not shout about its history. It just gilds it, sets it among the trees, and lets you climb up to meet it.
