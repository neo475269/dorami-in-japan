Beppu: Umi Jigoku

別府・海地獄

Umi Jigoku is the colour of a swimming pool in a dream — this impossible cobalt blue, steaming so hard the whole pond looks like it is boiling under a blanket of fog. The air smells like sulphur and wet leaves, and the wooden sign by the railing says this water is 98 degrees, which is exactly why nobody is allowed to dip a toe in, no matter how tempting it looks.

I bought an onsen egg from the little shop, still warm in its shell, and stood there peeling it while the steam curled around my sleeves. An older lady next to me laughed at how carefully I was holding it — like it was a treasure, not breakfast — and then showed me how to crack it in one clean tap. Hers came out perfect. Mine came out… enthusiastic. Still delicious.

Beppu has seven of these hells, and this one is called Sea Hell because the water looks like the sea decided to move inland and heat itself up. I stayed until the mist thinned and the blue went almost turquoise in the sun. Some places you visit; this one felt like it was cooking something just for me.
