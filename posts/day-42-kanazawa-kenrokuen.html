42

Kanazawa: Kenrokuen

金沢・兼六園

Kenrokuen is the kind of garden that makes you forget what time it is. I came in with my sketchbook to draw the pines by the Kasumigaike pond, and instead I just sat there for an hour, watching the light move across the water and the yukitsuri ropes cradling the branches, like the trees were wearing hand-knitted sweaters.

That's how I met Minako. She stopped beside me, looked at my very average sketch of the Kotoji lantern, and said — very gently, like she was telling me a secret — that the best angle was a few steps to the left. She was right, of course. She's from Kanazawa, works part-time at a little tea house in Higashi Chaya, and knows this garden the way you only know a place you've grown up in. We walked the whole garden together, her pointing out the smallest things — a maple leaf caught in a stone lantern, the carp gathering where the water is warmest — and me asking approximately a hundred questions.

By the time the afternoon light turned gold, we'd decided to travel together for a few days. She's heading to Shirakawa-go to visit her grandmother for the winter, and I'm heading that way anyway. After forty-one days of talking to myself, it feels strange and lovely to suddenly have someone to laugh with over the same view. Kanazawa, you've already given me more than I expected.
