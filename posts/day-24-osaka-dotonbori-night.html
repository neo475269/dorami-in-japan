Osaka: Dotonbori night

道頓堀 — グリコの看板

Dotonbori at night smells like grilled octopus and sweet sauce, and the whole canal glows — neon signs stacked on neon signs, their colours sliding across the water every time a boat goes past. I stood on Ebisu Bridge with a paper tray of takoyaki going cold in my hands because I was too busy staring up at the Glico running man.

The takoyaki man saw me juggling the tray and my phone and laughed, then piled on extra bonito flakes 'for the photo' — he didn't know I wasn't even taking one. We ended up chatting in a mix of my clumsy Japanese and his enthusiastic gestures, and he told me the best time to come is just after the rain, when the canal doubles every sign.

Standing there with sauce on my fingers and the whole noisy, glittering street humming around me, I felt completely, happily anonymous — just one more girl in the crowd, eating too fast, grinning at a giant neon runner. Osaka, I think I'm going to like you.
