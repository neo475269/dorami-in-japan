DAY
47

Nagano: Zenkoji

善光寺

Zenkoji's main hall is the biggest wooden building I have ever stood in front of, and it does not ask you to hurry — it just waits at the top of the steps while the pigeons do their rounds below. I bought a small bag of feed from a stall and spent a ridiculous amount of time on the stone steps, hand out, while the boldest pigeon marched straight up to me like it owned the temple.

An old man in a flat cap stopped beside me and laughed, then showed me how to hold my hand flat so a pigeon would land properly. One did — a fat grey one with green shining on its neck — and its feet were so light I almost laughed out loud. We stood there feeding pigeons together without exchanging a word beyond his smile, and somehow that was a whole conversation.

Inside the hall, the air smells like incense and old wood, and the quiet feels older than any quiet I know. I lit a candle and thought about how far I have come since Asakusa — forty-seven days of trains and temples and strangers who became friends for an afternoon. Nagano, you are cold and bright and kind, and I am glad I climbed your steps slowly.
