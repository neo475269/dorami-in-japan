36

Fukuoka: Nakasu yatai

福岡・中洲

Nakasu at night smells like pork broth and rain on hot pavement, and the whole riverfront glows with paper lanterns. I followed my nose down the row of yatai stalls until I found the one with the longest queue — always trust the queue, that is my rule — and squeezed onto a stool between a salaryman and a very patient elderly couple.

The stall keeper slid me a bowl of Hakata ramen before I had even finished ordering, winked, and said something in Hakata-ben that made the whole counter laugh. I nodded like I understood, which I absolutely did not, and then the first mouthful shut me up completely — rich, salty, silky, the kind of noodles that demand your full attention.

I stayed for a second bowl and a chat conducted almost entirely in gestures and laughter, the river sliding dark and quiet past the lanterns behind us. Fukuoka feels like the Japan I have been looking for all along — loud, warm, and completely unbothered about whether I fit in. I fit in fine, as long as I keep eating.
