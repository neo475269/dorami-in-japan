8

Nikko: Toshogu Shrine

日光東照宮 — 秋の陽明門

Nikko smells like cold cedar smoke and wet stone — I stepped off the train into air two degrees cooler than Tokyo, and the maple trees along the shrine approach were basically on fire.

I stood in front of the Yomeimon gate with my neck craned back like a tourist pigeon, trying to count the dragons, when a volunteer ojii-san in a happi coat tapped my elbow and whispered, "Look up — the white dragon protects the gate." Then he grinned and added, "I protect it too, but I only get a whistle."

Four hundred years of gold leaf and carvings, and this place still feels alive — like it isn't just remembering history, it's still showing off. Eight days in, and Japan keeps making me look up.
